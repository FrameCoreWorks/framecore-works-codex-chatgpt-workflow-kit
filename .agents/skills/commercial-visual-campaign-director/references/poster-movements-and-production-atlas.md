# Poster Movements and Production Atlas

Adapted for the existing Workflow Kit role. Source: FrameCore Works,
Static Graphic Design Creator, Apache-2.0, commit
`c3cbdf766411ca7e1131bc034bfa74ebad2efe71`. Ownership and links were

Use this detailed reference when a brief asks for a named poster movement or school, a print-process look, a film, theatre, social, or political poster, or a recommendation that needs more than the compact decision atlas. It is a decision aid, not a catalogue of presets.

Read the compact [poster style and composition atlas](poster-style-and-composition-atlas.md) first. This document expands its historical, functional, and production reasoning without changing its core rule: **a communication problem is resolved before a visual language is selected.**

## Scope and evidence status

The atlas documents selected European, Polish, Russian/Soviet and United States examples, plus contemporary operating categories. It does not contain dedicated Cuban, Indian or Japanese lineage cards; requests relying on those histories need specific source research rather than extrapolation. It is intentionally not a complete global history. Do not infer that an omitted region, movement, or practitioner is less important.

Use the labels below precisely:

- **Historical record** describes an origin, period, or documented practice.
- **Design synthesis** translates that record into an original, usable design mechanism.
- **Generator constraint** describes a raster-generation or production limitation that must receive QA.
- **Editorial rule** is a safety or quality boundary for this workflow.

Never turn a historical school into a bag of decorative tokens, a named artist into an imitation target, or a physical print process into a generic `vintage` filter.

## Decision path

Resolve these fields in order. A requested style is an input to discuss, not a substitute for the first six decisions.

```text
communication_goal
→ required_audience_response
→ reading_mode + text_load
→ visual_thesis
→ composition_archetype
→ image_type + type_role + hierarchy
→ historical_language_attributes
→ production_process + material treatment
→ exclusions + QA / DTP route
```

### Reading mode and copy hierarchy

Use qualitative viewing conditions rather than inventing universal seconds or print-distance numbers.

| Field | Values | Decision effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `reading_mode` | `glance`, `brief_scan`, `deliberate_read` | Sets how quickly the first message must resolve. |
| `must_read` | Meaning or action that cannot fail | Must survive reduction, contrast, and copy QA. |
| `should_read` | Useful supporting detail | Can be discovered after the first message. |
| `metadata` | Partners, legal detail, source, programme detail | Must not compete with the first message. |
| `decoration` | Non-semantic visual texture or rhythm | Must never impersonate information. |

`perceived_priority` is a design relation, not font size alone: relative scale, weight, contrast, position, negative space, and competing salience all matter. A face, glow, or high-contrast object can defeat a larger title.

**Generator constraint:** assess dates, prices, addresses, names, legal copy, schedules, credits and tables against the actual space and reading task in typography feasibility (the `image-prompting` handoff). Exactness requires QA; a data category alone does not set the risk level. Required metadata must remain readable. Do not solve this by default with a text-free background and a later overlay. Route a genuine `dtp_required` brief to the approved non-generative layout workflow before rendering.

### Non-automatic human decisions

Preserve supplied decisions and ask only when a material issue remains unresolved: political position, factual basis, charged symbol or claimed affiliation; intentional friction that threatens required reading; or an actual print process whose availability is unverified. Do not ask permission for routine reversible layout choices such as asymmetry. An approved experimental title can be expressive while required facts remain legible. Never fabricate evidence, documentary imagery, organisations, dates, statistics or attribution.

## Function-first profiles

Choose the communication function before choosing a movement or process.

| Function | First thing to resolve | Suitable mechanisms | Frequent failure | Default risk route |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Film | World, conflict, or interpretive hook | conceptual sign, narrative window, scale collision | floating heads and invented credits | protect title and real credits; route dense billing to DTP |
| Theatre or literary | Interpretive tension plus attendance facts | conceptual metaphor, hero word, controlled field | curtains, smoke, spotlights, or pseudo-cultural symbols | preserve title, date, venue |
| Social | Claim, evidence/symbol, and action | hero word, single sign, information beacon | emotive image replaces the message | verify claims and representation |
| Political | Position, actor/symbol, and action | hero word, figure, sign, purposeful photomontage | synthetic misinformation or historical-symbol misuse | require user-confirmed position and facts |
| Cultural event | Event identity and reason to attend | typographic scene, conceptual object, modular series | decorative artiness with a lost date | `brief_scan` to `deliberate_read` |
| Commercial | Product/offer recognition, value, action | hero object, scale contrast, campaign system | generic product beauty shot or invented packaging | protect product and brand truth |
| Informational | Accurate orientation | asymmetric information field, column or modular grid | collage, pseudo-data, illegible hierarchy | dense content often `dtp_required` |
| Flyer | Event/offer plus close-reading details | hero word plus structured detail zone | image leaves no data zone | medium/high text load; preflight required |
| Key visual | Reusable campaign recognition system | master sign, modular system, repeatable image-type relation | one-shot composition cannot adapt | create a system, not a single locked layout |
| Cover | Promise, title, and recognition | hero word, object, figure, conceptual sign | front cover overloaded with metadata | protect title/author; consider spine continuity separately |

## Composition mechanisms

Select one primary attention mechanism. A deliberate hybrid is valid when its component roles and reading order remain explicit.

| Mechanism | What it does | Strong use | Guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `hero_object` | Isolates one object or sign for recognition | commercial, cover, civic | The object must carry a real thesis, not occupy empty space. |
| `hero_word` | Makes one word or claim the visual event | manifesto, music, action | Functional details require a separate, quieter level. |
| `asymmetric_information_field` | Creates an ordered entry path through unequal zones | culture, information, flyer | Asymmetry must organise reading rather than merely scatter content. |
| `photomontage_scale_collision` | Uses source hierarchy and scale contrast to make an argument | film, political, editorial | Each fragment needs a semantic role; no random montage. |
| `diagonal_field` | Uses directional force and scale shifts to enact urgency | sport, action, music | Keep the essential message out of directional conflict. |
| `modular_series` | Repeats a governing rule across formats or instances | key visual, campaign, programme | Do not mistake repeatability for sameness. |
| `typographic_scene` | Lets type become image, rhythm, or spatial event | music, experimental culture | State the allowed legibility friction before designing. |
| `narrative_window` | Uses a crop or scene to reveal a world | film, editorial culture | Avoid generic stock staging and surplus characters. |
| `information_beacon` | Elevates one datum and groups secondary facts | alert, deadline, community action | Not suitable for a dense production-master layout. |

## Historical and formal language cards

Use one primary language plus a compatible process treatment when it helps the same communication mechanism. These are original attribute sets, never instructions to reproduce a particular poster, artist, or national stereotype.

### Art Nouveau — historical record: c. 1880s to First World War, international and regionally distinct

**Design synthesis:** use integrated image-type framing, sinuous contour, rhythmic ornament, and a deliberate figure or botanical silhouette when the message benefits from an enveloping decorative field.

**Strong fit:** cultural heritage, beauty, elegant event, editorial cover.

**Do not reduce it to:** a Mucha-like woman, a generic floral border, or a universal European look. Regional terms such as Jugendstil, Secession, Modernisme, and Stile Liberty are not interchangeable defaults.

### Art Deco and interwar artistic advertising — historical record: roughly 1910–1940

**Design synthesis:** stepped geometry, controlled axial tension, streamlined scale, decisive silhouettes, and display type that reinforces speed, travel, fashion, or modernity.

**Strong fit:** travel, fashion, premium gala, retro cinema, product recognition.

**Do not reduce it to:** black-and-gold luxury, an exoticised motif, or decorative period furniture. Check cultural references when the brief invokes colonial-era imagery.

### Sachplakat / object poster — historical record: early twentieth-century German commercial poster

**Design synthesis:** one actual product or sign, isolated and named with ruthless reduction. Recognition comes from object-message compression, not from generic minimalism.

**Strong fit:** product launch, retail recognition, compact commercial message.

**Do not reduce it to:** a fake retro product poster. The product must be the visual thesis and must use an approved product-truth asset when real.

### Constructivist mechanisms — historical record: 1920s Russian/Soviet and European avant-garde contexts

**Design synthesis:** directional type, diagonal energy, hard geometric planes, photo-fragment scale shifts, and assembled contrast that makes a specific argument.

**Strong fit:** urgency, sport, music, campaign, political or editorial work with an explicit user-approved position.

**Do not reduce it to:** red/black shorthand, propaganda tokens, or random cut-outs. Historical symbols and political messaging require human confirmation.

### New Typography — historical record: broader Central European 1920s–1930s information and typographic practice

**Design synthesis:** asymmetric information zones, functional type, photography as structure, and a reading sequence governed by purpose rather than symmetry.

**Strong fit:** exhibition, public information, modern institution, flyer, programme.

**Do not reduce it to:** a `Bauhaus` sticker of primary-colour circles. New Typography and Bauhaus overlap historically but are not the same category.

### Bauhaus — historical record: a school and network, 1919–1933

**Design synthesis:** use only the relevant mechanism: disciplined geometry, material/form research, image-type integration, or functional structure.

**Strong fit:** design education, exhibition, architecture, structured culture.

**Do not reduce it to:** primary geometric shapes or a generic 1920s grid. State why the geometry helps the message.

### Swiss / International Typographic Style — historical record: Switzerland, especially 1940s–1960s

**Design synthesis:** precise type-photo relation, measured grid, controlled asymmetry, limited palette, and visible but not bureaucratic hierarchy.

**Strong fit:** information, conference, institutional culture, campaign system.

**Do not reduce it to:** Helvetica plus white space and red/black. Swiss Style is not every Swiss poster, and a grid is useful only when it improves reading, comparison, or series behaviour.

### Polish Poster School — historical record: primarily Polish postwar poster culture of the 1950s–1960s

**Design synthesis:** compressed metaphor, interpretive image, type-image tension, wit, and a deliberately authored relation rather than literal film or theatre illustration.

**Strong fit:** theatre, film, literature, culture, civic concept.

**Do not reduce it to:** a rough painted surface, surreal face, folk token, or imitation of a named Polish artist. Keep the historical label narrow and translate the mechanism into a new thesis.

### Postwar systemic modernism — operating category, not one historical school

**Design synthesis:** modularity, cropping, repeatable rules, image/sign systems, and campaign consistency across formats.

**Strong fit:** identity systems, programmes, exhibitions, key visuals.

**Do not reduce it to:** a generic `mid-century` preset. Name the repeatable relation, then test whether it survives a new format.

### Psychedelic poster language — historical record: 1960s–1970s counterculture, especially San Francisco music poster culture

**Design synthesis:** optically active type, fluid contour, saturated colour tension, and perceptual rhythm where slower decoding is a purposeful part of the experience.

**Strong fit:** music, community, era-specific cultural event, experimental work with declared `intentional_legibility_friction`.

**Do not reduce it to:** rainbow gradients, melting letters, or drug iconography. Avoid for urgent dates, prices, safety instructions, and high-density functional copy unless a clean hierarchy is separately protected.

### Pop and mass-reproduction language — historical record: postwar mass-media and consumer-image practice

**Design synthesis:** enlargement, repetition, commercial-code tension, controlled halftone logic, and ironic contrast between message and image.

**Strong fit:** retail, youth activation, cultural commentary, product recognition.

**Do not reduce it to:** comic-book dots, generic speech bubbles, or cheerful colour. Pop and Sachplakat may both be bold and product-led, but they are historically and structurally distinct.

### Contemporary authorial methods — historical record: diverse late twentieth-century to current practices

**Design synthesis:** rule-based modularity, procedural variation, expressive type as a scene, controlled rule-breaking, and explicit awareness of the delivery medium.

**Strong fit:** cultural series, experimental identity, key visual systems.

**Do not reduce it to:** living-artist imitation, random procedural noise, or trendy effects. State the rule, the variation range, and the communication purpose.

## Production and materiality

Materiality follows a process. The workflow may specify a **visual simulation** only; it must not claim that a raster image was physically printed by the process. Confirm physical production separately.

| Process or construction | What produces the appearance | Useful prompt-level evidence | False shortcut to reject |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lithography | grease/water planographic drawing and colour separations | drawn crayon or tusche character, separated broad fields, paper participation | sepia, scratches, and generic old-paper damage |
| Screenprint | opaque ink through stencil/mesh, one separation at a time | flat spot fields, purposeful overlaps, limited palette, local overprint | random grunge or global dirt |
| Risograph | digital stencil master and colour drums on absorbent stock | limited ink colours, transparent overlap, restrained local registration tolerance, paper interaction | mandatory neon, blur, or misregistration |
| Letterpress | relief surface and pressure | crisp relief-derived edges, direct type-image relation, optional subtle impression | distressed font or universal deep deboss |
| Photomontage | assembled photographic sources before reproduction | declared source hierarchy, meaningful crop collision, distinct image roles | equal-weight photo fragments |
| Collage | physically assembled material fragments | selected material contrast, purposeful edges, one governing composition | torn-paper brush across the whole surface |
| Offset | plate transfers through blanket to paper, normally managed separations | controlled reproduction, stock/profile-aware colour, deliberate screening | generic `offset texture` or random noise |
| Halftone | tonal image converted into a dot structure | dots tied to a photographic/reproductive role, controlled scale and colour relationship | the same dot overlay applied to every surface |

### Process-fit rules

- Name palette limits, paper interaction, registration, overprint, screen structure, or pressure only when the selected process explains them.
- A reproduction process does not automatically determine the poster's historical language; `halftone`, `offset`, or `riso` is not a period label.
- A physical production request with exact stock, ink count, bleed, trapping, separation, spot colour, overprint, or proofing requirements is `production_master` and therefore `dtp_required`.
- Use a visual simulation to communicate material character in a concept raster; do not promise machine-specific accuracy.

## Type, image, and accessibility guardrails

Choose the relationship before naming type attributes:

| Type role | Image role | Use when | Guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `hero` | support or field | title or claim is the primary event | functional details must remain separate |
| `informational` | support | orientation is the main task | prefer non-generative layout for dense content |
| `integrated` | co-authored visual partner | culture, music, cover, experimental work | protect exact spelling and accepted friction |
| `supportive` | hero carries meaning | product, object, or narrative anchor dominates | type must not become generic filler |

Transfer the accessibility principle, not an unsupported print rule: critical information must remain distinguishable from its field under intended viewing conditions. Do not assert screen WCAG ratios as a complete print specification. For QA, inspect the design at multiple reduced scales and confirm that `must_read → should_read → metadata` survives.

## Anti-slop diagnostic

Classify the failure before repairing it.

| Failure class | Observable symptom | Correct repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Technical | wrong copy, pseudo-text, broken brand/product, inconsistent small details | scoped QA or DTP route; do not treat semantic text as texture |
| Strategy | no visual thesis, fake hierarchy, arbitrary collage, era mixing | rebuild goal, thesis, composition, and language selection |
| Editorial | generic glow/chrome/neon/dust, regional stereotype, process-as-filter | remove or replace the element with a named job and process logic |
| Ethical | copied artist/work, unapproved likeness, loaded symbol, synthetic documentary claim | stop and obtain authority, facts, or a new original direction |

A single critical failure blocks acceptance. Use the QA asset (the `qa-iteration` handoff) to distinguish local repair from a failed strategy; never require two defects or use a longer negative list as a repair.

## Prompt and handoff fields

Use the canonical [workflow contract](static-design-direction.md). Put reading mode, thesis, composition, type-image relation, selected historical attributes and material/process decisions in `strategy`. Put each exact string, required flag and attention role in `copy.items`. Put text risk and the intended checks in `feasibility`. Preserve relevant inherited historical context in the original handoff and `concept.lock.source_context` when applicable; do not create another partially specified `poster_strategy` schema.

Translate those decisions into the unified prompt's semantic order. The record is an internal rationale and QA contract, not extra visible text, multi-pass assembly or mandatory headings for a narrow edit.
