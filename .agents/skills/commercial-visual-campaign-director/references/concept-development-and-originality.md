# Concept Development and Originality

Use for open creative mechanisms, a request for stronger originality, or materially different concepts. A directed layout, copy correction or approved premise does not need a new concept exercise. Work internally unless the user benefits from seeing options. This asset owns concept development; the composition atlas owns layout and the integrated copy asset owns wording and voice.

## Diagnose the communication problem

Distinguish source facts, audience observations, hypotheses and unknowns. Establish what the audience should notice, understand, feel or do, and what currently prevents that response. A category label such as premium, cinematic, surreal or bold is not a concept. Do not invent an audience insight or business advantage as research evidence.

Extract usable material: an actual product behavior, physical property, event ritual, place, constraint, contradiction, phrase, consequence or culturally relevant detail. A visual thesis states what happens in the graphic and how that relation carries the message. For information-first work, the mechanism may be a clear comparison or reading structure rather than metaphor.

## Get past first association

Briefly identify the obvious category images and slogans. Keep one only if it serves the communication problem better than the alternatives; novelty alone is not a goal. Find a more specific relation using a few relevant operations:

| Operation | Design question | Failure to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Consequence | Can the result of an action become the visible event? | Unsupported benefit or fake before/after evidence |
| Absence | Can the missing element expose the message? | Empty space with no meaning |
| Reversal | What changes when the expected relationship is reversed? | A puzzle unrelated to the objective |
| Scale or context | Which precise shift makes a property understandable? | Random giant objects or generic surrealism |
| Literal language | Can form enact a meaningful word or phrase? | Decorative wordplay that obscures the message |
| Typography as action | Can spacing, repetition, interruption or shape carry the thesis? | Unreadable mandatory copy |
| Product evidence | Can a true feature demonstrate the proposition? | Cosmetic product staging or invented specifications |
| Cultural observation | Does an audience-specific ritual create recognition? | Stereotypes, borrowed affiliation or fabricated research |

Do not exhaust a list or narrate hidden reasoning. Present concise conclusions, evidence, decisions and trade-offs.

## Build paired routes

For unresolved concept and wording, develop each route as a coherent proposal: message thesis, concrete visual mechanism, candidate headline/support/CTA when useful, relation between text and image, attention order, fit to audience/format, one trade-off and relevant factual uncertainties. Copy is governed by the existing copy-voice role (the `copy-voice` handoff).

Normally show two to four useful routes, honoring a different requested count. Differences must change the argument, audience entry point or mechanism; palette/font/effect changes alone are design variants. If copy is locked, every route preserves it. If the concept is locked, explore only allowed design decisions. A user can select the concept and exact wording together; do not request the same approval twice. Combining routes creates a new candidate only when it changes the selected mechanism or words materially.

## Evaluate before recommending

Compare relevance, specificity, comprehension, message ownership, text-image contribution and feasibility. Treat audience impact as a hypothesis unless observed evidence exists. Test replacing the brand/event/product in the complete text-image relation: what must change? For a type-led poster, deleting the title destroys the concept and is not a valid originality test.

Reject concepts that rely on unsupported claims, an interchangeable mood image, a familiar composition copied from a reference, an unrelated visual riddle, or effects with no communication job. Do not force metaphor onto a public-information brief or novelty onto an exact constrained edit. Recommend one route with a concrete reason and a meaningful trade-off; do not assign fake precision or guarantee conversion, uniqueness or exclusive ownership.

## Lock and preserve

Record the selected premise, mechanism, distinctive hook, allowed adaptations and forbidden substitutions in the Static Direction Contract (`concept.lock` in optional planning notation). `concept.status` is `needs_selection`, `supplied`, `selected`, `locked`, or `not_required` for a task that does not finalize a design concept. An approved brief may already supply the lock; do not force reapproval.

During production, change only the allowed crop, format, scale, supporting arrangement or other approved variables. If legibility or fidelity would require a different mechanism, explain that conflict and offer a bounded alternative. Preserve an optional upstream concept handoff when supplied; otherwise develop the concept within the existing static-direction role.

## Calibration examples

Fictional book-exchange brief: readers bring a finished book and choose another, without a fee. 'Discover a world of possibilities' has no exchange-specific premise. 'Your bookshelf already knows the ending.' uses a reader observation; a book leaving its shelf can make the exchange visible. 'Read by you. New to someone else.' uses a change of perspective and suggests two readers linked by one book. Neither candidate is selected until the user chooses; neither proves campaign effectiveness.

Directed one-word civic poster: the supplied word and deliberate surrounding absence can constitute the whole mechanism. Adding a crowd, icon, CTA and textured hero solely to fill the eight stages would weaken it.

Commercial product brief: if the only verified fact is a refillable container, explore the refill action or repeated use of the same vessel. Do not invent percentages, lifetime savings or environmental certification to make the concept persuasive.
